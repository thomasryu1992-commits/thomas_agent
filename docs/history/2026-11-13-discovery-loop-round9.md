# 2026-11-13 — discovery loop round 9: the form's size line in the ladder's units, and a second hypothesis below the first hundred

**Shipped (registration and tools; nothing measured in this commit):** `scripts/research/strategy_ladder.py`
(`skip_day_return`, `LowerTier`, the lower-tier book `L13`, `lower_counts`, `lower_run`),
`docs/proposals/STRATEGY_DISCOVERY_LOOP_V0.1.md` §23, one rule added to the hypothesis form.

**Why the form changes.** H-L1 wrote "about 10% a year" on its size line and went up a ladder whose entry rule asks
15% of total position. The form and the ladder were not connected. The size line is now written in the ladder's own
units, net of the cost the book will be judged on, with the cost arithmetic beside it; a size under the floor is
said to be under it and the prior follows.

**What that rule showed at once.** Written honestly, no new hypothesis on the data on disk has a size over the
floor. Things did clear it on the development years (the volatility cluster, the funding switch, M04); the first
two closed on the confirm span and M04 is in its registered screen. The floor is Thomas's and is not moved here;
the fact is reported to him.

**Why this hypothesis.** The loop's own order for a stall says: fix the failing rung, add a logic, widen the tier,
then hourly data, then ask for a source. Widening is next. Below the first hundred by volume the map has never been
read, and a small account is the one participant who can trade there, which is a reason for something to be left.
The candidate is the plainest thing with a payer: weekly reversal, long what fell and short what rose.

**Three choices that are not obvious.**

- *The rank skips a day.* The book trades at the open after the decision, which is the decision day's close. A
  rank on that close lets a last print at the bid pick the coin and then credits the bounce. The seven-day return
  ends a day earlier.
- *Flat time counts as zero.* The tier exists only once more than a hundred perps trade. Rung 2's five-year rule and
  rung 3 are read on all five years with the flat months and years as zeros; reading the active years alone would
  loosen the rule. H-L1's "active at 60% of decisions" stop would trip by construction, so the stop here is a floor
  of 26 active decisions in each of 2022, 2023 and 2024.
- *The judged verdict is expected to fail on cost, and the registration says so.* Full weekly turnover at 26 bps a
  unit is about 27% of total position a year. What the round can learn is in the p beside 300 weekly random books
  and in the 3 bps value. If those two say there is something and the stressed cost says it cannot be taken, one
  later read is allowed, once, with a cost table fixed from the spread record before any return is computed.

## The result (same day)

**What came out.** H-R1 failed every condition and is closed. At the judged cost the annual Sharpe is -0.674, and
28% of the weekly random books did as well or better; at 3 bps of slippage it is -0.081, so this is not the
cost-dependent case and the one re-read the registration allowed is not taken. Before costs and funding the book
earned about 7% of total position a year while it stood, costs took about 19%, and it paid funding on top.

**What the round leaves.** The tier below the first hundred is not thin: a book stands from August 2021 and almost
every week from 2022. The tool that builds it stays for later hypotheses. The size line written in the ladder's
units said beforehand that the verdict would fail on cost, and it did; what it could not say was that at 3 bps the
book would still not clear the entry rule, nor stand apart from random books. The price return itself was positive
(about 4% of total position a year after funding, while the book stood). The next step in the loop's own order is hourly data.
